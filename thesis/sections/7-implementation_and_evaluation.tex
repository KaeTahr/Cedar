\section{Evaluation and Results}
This chapter demonstrates how the Cedar prototype
performs on representative examples of C data-layout specifications.
It evaluates the language in terms of 
\textbf{expressiveness}, \textbf{correctness},
and \textbf{practicality}—the same goals that motivated its design.
Rather than formal experiments or extensive benchmarks, the evaluation
focuses on a small set of illustrative examples that reflect typical
systems-programming tasks: defining packed records, illustrative examples,
and tagged unions.
For each example, Cedar layouts were written, checked, and
translated into C code, and the outputs were compiled with a standard
C compiler to verify that they are syntactically valid and
structurally correct.

We continue by describing the evaluation process and criteria.
Later, we present examples, results and analysis, followed by a short
discussion of limitations and possible extensions.

\subsection{Evaluation Methodology}
The evaluation is designed to show that Cedar's declarative layout
model works coherently for real C constructs and to identify its
current limitations. Because Cedar is a prototype language rather than a
production compiler, the assessment emphasizes \textbf{correctness}
and \textbf{tool behaviour} rather than raw performance.
